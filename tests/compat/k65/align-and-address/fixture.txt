**`align` and `address` are accepted at the start of a code section, and
`align N + M` means "at an address congruent to M modulo N".**

The golden places `offset_table` at `$2004` (`align 16 + 4`), `aligned` at
`$2008` (`align 256 + 8`) and `addressed` at `$2040` (`address 0x2040`), and
zero-fills the gaps.

`Align` sits between `SecStart` and the body in `func`, `naked`, `main` *and*
`data` (`compiler.inc:121-136`), which answers the question probe 4 left as
"unverified".  `setalign(a, offs)` (`compiler_src.cpp:877`) then requires `a`
to be a power of two in `1..65536` and `offs` to be less than `a`, and marks
every start address that is not congruent to `offs` unusable.

Since G1 nothing here is a syntax error.  The grammar has both forms - the
offset (`align N + M`, its own alternative over atoms, because the `+` is not
the expression operator) and the placement directives at the head of a code
section - and each announces itself as `K816-E0201` until G2 lowers it.  The
two `K816-E0104` are the `a = 1` and `a = 2` in sections whose width the
refused directives left unknown.

What G1 deliberately did *not* do is let `align 16 + 4` compile.  Before this
task K816 folded the sum and emitted `.align $14`, which places the section at
a multiple of twenty - a different address from either reading of the source,
and emitted without a word.  Refusing it is the correction; decision 15's
lowering (`.align N` then `.res M`) is G2's.  Decision 15's lowering - `.align N` followed by
`.res M` - puts the label at the same congruence class, and differs from the
original in emitting M filler bytes where the original leaves the linker a gap.
That difference is `backend-different`, and G2 lands it.
