**A bare identifier at the start of a statement is a call, and the line does
not end the statement.**  `Instr -> Arg` (`compiler.inc:154`) and
`dofunction`: `stamp_one slot = a` is a call to the `inline` and then a store,
which the golden shows as `85 81 85 80` - `STA one`, `STA slot`, in that order.
`stamp_two stamp_one` on one line is two calls, `85 82 85 81`.  K816 emits
every one of those bytes in the same order; the two images differ in one place
and it is the linker's.

This row was `open` from G0 until G10a, and the reason is worth keeping: K816
gave the *first* identifier of a statement to its address-encoding prefix rule.
`dp`, `abs` and `far` take the operand that follows them, and the grammar
cannot see which spelling a prefix used - so every bare call with anything
after it on the line reached `K816-E0001`, which is a valid K65 statement
getting a generic parse error.  Decision 16 called it a grammar defect and G1
fixed it in the AST builder, where the spelling *is* visible
(`postmortem.md` §G.1.8); the row stayed open because nothing had measured the
original's answer.  Now something has.

Three shapes, and the third is the one that says what rule is *not* being
introduced: a call alone on its line, a call with a store after it, and two
calls on one line.  K65 is newline-insensitive and stays so - the fix narrows
the prefix, it does not end a statement at the newline.

The original inlines each expansion in place and labels it `__I<n><name>`;
K816 has no label of its own for an expansion.  Neither emits a byte for the
label, so the sequences agree.

That one difference: the original opens with `4C 04 20`, a `JMP main` stub K816
does not emit because ld65 places the entry, so `answers` sits at `$2000` here
and `$2003` there and the closing `LDA` names it accordingly.  Every generated
byte between agrees.

`a = slot` opens the body rather than an immediate, deliberately.  The original
has no `#`: `Arg -> Number` *is* the immediate (`:269`), and `a = #MARK` does
not merely fail to parse there - it makes the compiler spin, which is worth
knowing and is not this row's subject.
