row       var-declaration
config    atarixl.cfg
compare   bytes

**`?` prints the variable's address at compile time and emits nothing.**  The
golden is `A5 80 A5 81 A5 82 A9 07` - three zero-page loads and an immediate -
and the compiler prints one extra line while building it:

    Variable 'cntY' located at $82

`Var -> Ident Verbose` (`compiler.inc:36-41`) passes the marker through to
`var(...)` as its `verbose` argument, and the only thing it reaches is that
`printf` (`compiler_src.cpp:446`).  K816 accepting and ignoring it (decision
13) moves no byte, which is what the golden had to show.

Since G1 this fixture **compiles**, so it compares bytes rather than
diagnostics.  Before G1 it produced six diagnostics - two syntax errors where
the commas were, and four undeclared names as a consequence.

`expected.hex` is K816's, and it is not the golden's `A5 80 A5 81 A5 82 A9 07`:
`anim` keeps its `$80`, but `cntX` and `cntY` are linker-allocated, and
`atarixl.cfg` gives the linker one segment, so they land at `$2000`/`$2001` -
inside the code - and the loads are absolute.  Where an unaddressed variable
goes is the link configuration's answer, not the language's; the row this
fixture serves is the comma list and the `?` marker, and on those the two
compilers agree.

Two more things the golden settles:

  - **`var NAME = N` gives an address, not an initial value.**  `anim` is at
    `$80` because the declaration says so, and `cntX` and `cntY` follow it at
    `$81` and `$82` - the allocator puts each next variable right after the
    previous one.  The three loads are `A5 80`, `A5 81`, `A5 82`.
  - **A `const` with no value is legal until it is read.**  `const FIRST,
    SECOND = 7` declares both; `FIRST` is only an error at a use site
    ("Constant 'FIRST' doesn't have value assigned").

Not asked here, and worth knowing before writing more fixtures: `A`, `X`, `Y`,
`S` and the flag letters `c C d D i I o O` are *tokens*, not identifiers
(`compiler.inc:244-260`), so `const A` is a syntax error in the original, and
so is `var v`.
