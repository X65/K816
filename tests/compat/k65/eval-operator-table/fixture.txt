**Thirty-two operators, and the two compilers differ in one byte.**

Every entry carries a value worked out by hand and the golden is the arbiter.
Laid side by side, the data bytes are:

    oracle   07 09 05 04 01 02 10 10 08 0F 06 FB FE 01 00 01 01 00 00
    K816     07 09 05 04 01 02 10 10 08 0F 06 FA FE 01 00 01 01 00 00
                                           ^^
    oracle   01 00 05 03 01 00 0B 16 03 | 07 | 02 | 03
    K816     01 00 05 03 01 00 0B 16 03 | 07 | 02 | 03

The one difference is `~5 & 255`: 251 there, 250 here.  It is the same defect
twice over, and it is worth spelling out because it is the reason a bitwise
operand needed a conversion of its own.

`EvalAny::ToInt` is `int(floor(x) + .5f)` (`eval.h:28`).  For a non-negative
value that is `floor(x)`; for a negative one, `floor(x) + 0.5` crosses back
toward zero and the truncation lands one *too high*.  So `(0 - 2) | 0` is -1
there, `(0 - 1) << 4` is 0, and here `~5` is -6 but the `&` that follows
re-converts it and gets -5.  K816 truncates toward zero throughout, which
agrees with the original everywhere the original agrees with itself: the
thirteenth byte, `(0 - 2.7) & 255`, is `FE` in both.

Three readings the documents did not have, all of them in the bytes above:

  - **`%` is a non-negative modulo.**  `fmod(fmod(a,b)+b,b)`, so `(0-7) % 3`
    is 2 and not -1 - the sixth byte.
  - **`&&` does not short-circuit.**  The stack machine's `LOGBINARY` pops two
    values that were both computed, so an operand with an effect has it either
    way.
  - **A run of statements is one value.**  `assignments`, `compounds` and
    `steps` are one bracket each and one byte each, because the original
    separates statements with the *pop* operator: everything before the last
    is evaluated and discarded.

Two spellings are deliberately absent and live in
`tests/compat/k816/eval-extensions/` instead, because the original does not
have them: `||`, which is not a token there at all, and the prefix `++`/`--`,
which abort the compiler.
