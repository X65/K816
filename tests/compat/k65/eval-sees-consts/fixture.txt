**The original's evaluator sees `const` names, and sees a data label as a
section.**  `[FLAG_A]` is `$30`, `[FLAG_A | FLAG_B]` is `$3C`, and
`[index(tab, 2)]` is `30`, the third byte of `tab`.

`getevalvar` (`compiler_src.cpp:288`) resolves a name against the *one* symbol
table the compiler keeps: a `const` yields its value, a label yields the
section it names, and an evaluator variable yields whatever was assigned to it.
A `var` is the one kind that does not resolve - `[vx]` for a declared `var vx`
is "Incorrect type of eval variable".

This settles two things decision 4 left open.  K816 letting a bracket read a
`const` is **compatibility, not an extension** - the row is `compatible` - and
the legacy K816's dual parse, which existed because *its* evaluator could not
see `const`, has nothing left to rescue and is not reproduced.

Since G6 all four brackets compile, and the fixture compares bytes: `30 3C 1E`
against the original's `30 3C 1E`, byte for byte, with `index(tab, 2)` the
third of them.  `tests/compat/k816/eval-sees-consts-compiled/` keeps the
narrower case it was written for.

Two differences from the oracle image, both the *linker's* and neither a byte
either compiler generated.  The original opens the image with `4C 06 20` - a
`JMP main` stub K816 does not emit, because ld65 places the entry - and it
drops `tab` entirely, since nothing references it; ld65 keeps a section that is
in the link.  So K816's image carries `0A 14 1E 28` where the original's
carries the stub, and every generated byte after that agrees.

The fourth bracket is also what pins the *operand* reading.  `a = [ FLAG_B ]`
is `A9 0C` in the golden - an **immediate**, because `Arg -> Number` is
`arg(AM_IMM, ...)` (`compiler.inc:270`) and `Number -> [ EvalCode ]`.  K816 read
it as an address until G4 measured this line.
