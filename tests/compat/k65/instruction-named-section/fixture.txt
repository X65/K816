**A section may be named after an instruction in the original, and not
here — because of what K816 writes, not what it reads.**

The original's grammar has no raw mnemonics: `Instr -> Arg` and the HLA
forms are the whole of a statement, so `lda` is an ordinary identifier.
`oracle.lst` has `section lda` and `JSR lda`, and `oracle.hex` links it —
eleven bytes, the bare call reaching the section by name.

K816 emits ca65 assembly, and there `lda:` at the start of a line is the
instruction: the assembler answers "Expected an expression but found ':'"
and refuses the file.  So a name K816 accepted would produce a program that
cannot be assembled, with nothing said until ca65 speaks about a file the
author did not write.  `K816-E0173` says it where the name is.

The second code is the other half of the same sentence, and worth keeping in
the fixture rather than writing the refusal alone.  Once `lda` cannot be a
section, the bare `lda` on its own line in `main` is the *instruction* — and
the 65816 has no implied `LDA`, so `K816-E0106` says it does not support that
operand form.  The original reads the same line as a call (`JSR lda`); the two
readings are exactly what the row is about.

The narrowing is the whole unit-level namespace and not only sections:
`var sec`, `data and` and `naked jmp` reach the same emitted shapes.
`const bit` happens to survive ca65 today — a `const` is exported inline as
`.export bit : abs = $01` rather than written at the start of a line — and is
refused anyway, because "some instruction names may be declared and others
may not" is a rule nobody can hold in their head, and because what a `const`
emits is K816's to change.

Two namespaces are *not* narrowed, exactly as they are not for a register
spelling: an `inline` parameter and an evaluator variable.  Neither is ever
emitted, so neither can collide with anything — `inline mask (a, #and)` and
`[ nop = 3 ]` both compile.
