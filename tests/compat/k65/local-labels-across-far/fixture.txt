**The second of the two `doc/k65/known-bugs.md` items that touch K816, and it
does not reproduce.**  The sentence is "Local labels can't be accessed across
certain language constructs (like far calls).  Local labels before and after
such construct appear to be in different local namespaces."  At the revision
`oracle.lock` pins, both directions resolve - `oracle.lst`:

    main
    __local0_top
        DEX
        JMP     __farjmp_core___local0_rest
    __local0_rest
        BNE     __local0_top

One namespace, one `local_index`, and the far jump between them changes
nothing.  The same holds for a far *call*, measured the same way.

**Where the original's local namespace really does split is a section
boundary**, and nowhere else: `Ident -> . IdentText` mangles the name to
`__local<N>_`, and `N` is incremented by `makesection` (`compiler_src.cpp:818`)
- that is, at the end of every section, not at any construct inside one.  So
the bug as written cannot happen for a construct that does not end a section,
and neither `far` form does.  It is recorded as not reproducible rather than
as fixed: nothing in K816 closed it, and a source that once tripped it would
have to say which construct did.

**K816's answer is the same shape by a different route.**  A `.name` is a ca65
cheap-local scoped to the enclosing function, so `@top` and `@rest` are one
scope whatever stands between them, and `far goto` is a `jml` rather than a
trampoline into a generated stub section.  `BNE` reaches back across the long
jump, which is the claim: one namespace, whatever the construct between the
two labels is.

**G8 removed the `jml`.**  It is a jump to the label written on the very next
line, which is pass 9, and this fixture said it would notice.  `expected.hex`
is `CA D0 FD 60` now, four bytes rather than eight: `DEX`, `BNE` back to it,
`RTS`.  The name-resolution claim is untouched - `@rest` is still declared
after the `far goto` that names it, and `@top` is still named from after it -
and what the bytes stopped demonstrating is only that the two labels were once
five bytes apart.
