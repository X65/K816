**`* N` waits N *cycles*, and it spends an odd one on an illegal opcode.**  The
golden is

    EA           *          one NOP, two cycles
    04 80 EA     * 5        NOP $80 (three cycles) then NOP (two)
    EA EA EA     * 6        three NOPs
    04 80        %          NOP $80 - three cycles, not BRK
    B8           o-         CLV
    68 48 28 08  a?? a!! z?? z!!

`opnop` (`compiler_src.cpp:540`) refuses a negative wait, refuses `* 1` as
unrealizable, refuses more than 256, and spends an odd cycle through
`NOP $80` - the 6502's undocumented three-cycle `NOP zp`, **opcode `$04`**.

That last byte is the whole argument for decision 11.  On the 65816 `$04` is
`TSB dp`, a real instruction that reads and writes memory, so the original's
odd-cycle wait is not merely different on this target - it cannot be
reproduced.  Compatibility here is not cheap, it is impossible, so K816 keeps
its own meaning for `* N` and `%` (`doc/syntax-reference.md`: N NOPs, and
`BRK`), `--strict-k65` warns, and the row is `intentionally-changed` with this
golden as its evidence.

`o-` is the other half of the fixture and needs no argument: `Flag -> o` is the
original's spelling of the overflow flag (`compiler.inc:259-260`) and `B8` is
`CLV`, so decision 14's alias is `compatible`.  Its opposite is not: `o+`
assembles to `SEv`, which does not exist, and the original compiler dies
without a diagnostic.  That is a `legacy-defective` row, and K816 refusing
`o+` is the right behaviour rather than a compatibility break.
