**A bracket above a directive runs before it, and what a live condition
assigns outlives the directive.**

Two questions about order, answered by `oracle.hex` in five bytes:

  - `01 42` — the `[ FLAG = 1 ]` entry emitted its value and assigned `FLAG`,
    and the `#if FLAG` three lines below it then took the true branch. Had
    the directive been decided first, `FLAG` would have had no value and the
    byte would be `24`.
  - `A9 03` — `#if [ MODE = 3 ]` assigned `MODE`, and `a = [ MODE ]` below
    the `#endif` reads it. The condition goes through the same `doeval` as
    any other bracket, against the same variable table.

Both were wrong in K816 when G5 first landed, and both for the same reason:
the pass rewrote a whole item — descending into it, deciding every directive
inside — and only then ran the item's brackets. Liveness and evaluation are
one source-order walk or they are neither. `postmortem.md` §G.6.7.

The second one costs the design something. A condition is evaluated by
`preprocess.rs` and by nothing else, since the directive is deleted before
analysis begins — so unlike a bracket it has no second run to declare what it
assigned or to report what it printed, and the pass has to carry both across.
That is `preprocess::Effect`, and §G.6.8.

`expected.hex` is K816 producing the same five bytes.
