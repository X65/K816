**One warning, and the three sections around it are why it is only one.**

A `->` clause draws the line between a *result* and damage.  Registers have
been on that line since sub-plan E; flags are on it now, and the whole
observable difference is which side the carry falls on.

`check` declares `-> a, c`, so the `c-?` after its call site is reading an
answer and nothing is reported.  `silent` has the identical body and declares
`-> a`, so the same `c-?` is reading a flag the callee never promised - which
is the warning, and it names `N Z C` because a compare writes all three.

That is the point of the pair: the two bodies are the same instructions.  The
only thing that changed is what the clause claims, which is what makes this a
*language* rule rather than an analysis heuristic.

`narrows` is the third case and it reports nothing.  A width-only clause
declares an exit width, not a register flow: `FunctionInfo::declared_outputs`
is `None` for it, and it makes nothing undefined.  If it recorded an empty set
instead, every register and flag would read as undefined after the call and
this fixture would carry a fourth diagnostic.

`call foo` is not exercised here because it opts out of the check entirely,
which `doc/syntax-reference.md` documents and G9b did not change.  The
undefinedness is still recorded for the optimizer at every call site; the
*warning* is what the escape hatch escapes.
