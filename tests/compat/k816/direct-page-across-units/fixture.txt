**Decision 24's first judgment, measured: the frontend stays as it is.**  The
question sub-plan E left open (`plans/04-hla-and-lowering.md`, "Out of scope")
was whether the direct-page pool should be a linker-level resource rather than
a frontend one, "against the corpus, once decision 13 has told us how much
`dp` real programs ask for".

**What the corpus asks for is nothing.**  No K65 source in any corpus we hold
uses `dp` at all - the pool is K816's, not K65's - and in the legacy K816's
316 fixtures the largest ask outside a deliberate overflow test is a handful
of pointers.  There is no program whose real claim comes near a page.

**The cross-unit half is already a linker resource, and ld65 already catches
it with the same arithmetic.**  The one cross-unit overflow case in the legacy
corpus is `link-dp-overflow-err`: 200 bytes in one unit, 100 in another, each
fitting alone.  The legacy K816 was its own linker and reported "100 bytes
requested, 56 bytes free out of 256" against the declaration.  K816 compiles
one unit at a time and hands the claims to ld65 as `ZEROPAGE` reservations,
which answers `Segment 'ZEROPAGE' overflows memory area 'ZP' by 44 bytes` -
the same 44 bytes, named as a segment rather than as a variable.

So the split is not an accident of where the code sits.  What a *unit* asks
for is a fact the frontend knows, and `K816-E0133` reports it there; what a
*link group* asks for is not - `--unit` is a name-visibility list, and nothing
requires it to be the set of objects ld65 is given.  A frontend check over the
workspace would miss overflows it cannot see and could refuse programs that
are never linked together.  Moving the check would trade a right answer for a
guess.

This fixture is the positive half: two units, two claims, one page, and
one-byte operands on both sides of the boundary.  The row is
`K816-extension`, because the pool is not in the original's grammar at all.
