**The part of `k65/eval-integer-boundary` where the two compilers must agree
exactly, checked at the bytes.**

    FF FF 02 03 03 FE FE FD 01 02 03 04 04 00 01 F9 01

The oracle fixture cannot make this claim itself: six of its twenty-two entries
are out of range, the original wraps them silently and K816 refuses them, so
it stops at the diagnostics and sixteen agreements go unchecked.  These are
those sixteen, in the oracle's order, and each is the byte
`k65/eval-integer-boundary/oracle.hex` has for the same entry.  The last is
not in the oracle fixture: it is the hexadecimal twin of the fifteenth.

Five of them are negative, and they were refused until sub-plan P: a value
fits a byte when it lies in -128 ... 255, emitted as its low eight bits
(`plans/15-constant-sizes.md` decision 2), so `[0 - 1]` is `FF` here for the
same reason `const EUNKNOWN = -1` is `$FF` in an 8-bit immediate.

Read in order they are the whole numeric model:

  - **The rounding is `floor(v + 0.5)`** - half *up*, not away from zero and
    not to even.  `2.5`, `1.5` and `3.5` all round upward, and so does `7 / 2`,
    which is float division that happens to land on a half.
  - **The arithmetic is `f64` and both ends are `f32`.**  The tenth byte is
    zero because `16777217` and `16777216` are the *same literal*: a decimal
    literal goes through `ParseFloat` and is a `float`
    (`compiler_eval.cpp:172`).  The eleventh is one because `0x1000001` and
    `0x1000000` do not: a literal beginning `0x` or `0b` goes through
    `ParseInt` and is exact.
  - So `EvalExprKind::Number` carries whether its spelling was exact.  Nothing
    downstream of the decoded value could have told, which is why the flag is
    in the AST and not derived later.

What is *not* copied is the wrapping, and that is decision 4's one deliberate
difference: K816 keeps its range check, so a bracket that produces 300 in a
byte entry is `K816-E0109`, exactly as the literal `300` is.  A negative value
is not wrapped either; it is in range.
