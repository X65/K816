**The half of `k65/eval-integer-boundary` where the two compilers must agree
exactly, checked at the bytes.**

    FF 02 03 03 01 02 03 04 04 00 01

The oracle fixture cannot make this claim itself: eleven of its twenty-two
entries are out of range, the original wraps them silently and K816 refuses
them, so it stops at the diagnostics and eleven agreements go unchecked.
These are those eleven, and each byte here is the byte at the same position in
`k65/eval-integer-boundary/oracle.hex`.

Read in order they are the whole numeric model:

  - **The rounding is `floor(v + 0.5)`** - half *up*, not away from zero and
    not to even.  `2.5`, `1.5` and `3.5` all round upward, and so does `7 / 2`,
    which is float division that happens to land on a half.
  - **The arithmetic is `f64` and both ends are `f32`.**  The tenth byte is
    zero because `16777217` and `16777216` are the *same literal*: a decimal
    literal goes through `ParseFloat` and is a `float`
    (`compiler_eval.cpp:172`).  The eleventh is one because `0x1000001` and
    `0x1000000` do not: a literal beginning `0x` or `0b` goes through
    `ParseInt` and is exact.
  - So `EvalExprKind::Number` carries whether its spelling was exact.  Nothing
    downstream of the decoded value could have told, which is why the flag is
    in the AST and not derived later.

What is *not* copied is the wrapping, and that is decision 4's one deliberate
difference: K816 keeps its range check, so a bracket that produces 300 in a
byte entry is `K816-E0109`, exactly as the literal `300` is.
