**A far call inside an `inline` is the same call at every expansion.**
`k65/far-call-in-inline` measures what the original does with the shape:
`link_invoke_far_call` generates a `__far_<from>_<to>__<name>` trampoline and
takes `<from>` from the section the call is *written* in, which for an
`inline` is the inline itself.  The stub is decided once, when the body is
read, and copied into every expansion - so an expansion in another bank
returns with the wrong bank selected.  That is the
`doc/k65/known-bugs.md` item, and `oracle.lst` beside that fixture is it.

**The construct the bug lives in does not exist here.**  `bank` is removed
(decision 10) and `far` is the 65816's 24-bit addressing, so a far call is
`jsl` to a 24-bit address and a far section returns `rtl`.  There is no
trampoline to name, no "from" bank to get wrong, and nothing about the
expansion site that could change the encoding: `expected.hex` shows the same
`JSL $002000` three times, from two different functions.

**What is not free, and is not a defect.**  An un-annotated `inline` knows
nothing about its use site, so a call inside it bridges to the callee's entry
contract defensively; annotating the section - or, as here, the module -
removes the bridge, because the body is classified once against its own
contract (`doc/syntax-reference.md`, "Why aliases are inline-only").  There is
no `sep`/`rep` in these bytes for that reason, and one appearing would mean
the contract stopped reaching the expansion.
