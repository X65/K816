**A `.kk` source is K816, and K816 writes four of K65's forms its own way.**
The postfix loop is `loop { x-- } while !=`, the signed flag test `c?`, the
overflow relation `v?` and the stack shorthand `push a`, and each K65
spelling here is refused with a fix that writes the K816 one.  The same
four lines in a `.k65` source compile without a word, and to the bytes the
fixed `.kk` compiles to - `tests/dialect.rs` applies every fix and compares
them - so the dialect decides what is refused and never what anything
means.

The rule is one of spelling, which is why it lives in `src/spelling.rs`
beside the push-width checks rather than in the grammar: the grammar is one
for both dialects (master plan decision 2), so an old spelling in a `.kk`
parses, and the refusal can name it and offer the fix rather than report
`expected a statement`.
