**Regions nest at three levels, and `#elif` works at every one of them.**

The original gives all three levels the same productions and then aborts on
an `#elif` at the two inner ones (`k65/preprocessor-elif-inner-levels`), so
there is no oracle to compare against here: this is K816's own claim, and the
bytes are the assertion.

Each chain picks a branch nothing else could produce:

  - the top-level chain falls through `#if [FEATURE == 0]` to
    `#elif [TIER == 2]`, so `MARK` is `$E1`;
  - the data block's first chain falls through two dead branches to
    `#elif FEATURE`, so the first byte is `$12`;
  - the `#else` of a negated condition gives `$21`;
  - a region nested inside a live region gives `$31`;
  - the statement-level chain falls through two dead branches to `#else`,
    so `$F2` reaches `port`;
  - and the last region is inside a `nocross { }` block, which is a statement
    list of its own and therefore its own level, so `$41` reaches `slot`.

Every branch *stores* what it chose. A branch that only loaded a register
would be dropped as a dead store before it reached the bytes, and the fixture
would then pass whatever the preprocessor did.

The conditions deliberately read both namespaces a condition can read — a
`const` and a name a bracket assigned — and use the bracket form for anything
richer than a name, because that is the whole condition grammar
(`compiler.inc:23-25`, and `preprocess.rs` on why K816 does not widen it).
