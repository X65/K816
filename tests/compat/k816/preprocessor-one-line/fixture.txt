**A directive ends at the end of its line, and a bracket inside one may
still wrap.**

Three refusals, one per split directive, and nothing for the fourth region:
the bracket that opens on the `#if` line and closes two lines later is fine,
because a `]` says where it ends and no later line can be absorbed into it.

This is stricter than the original, which accepts all four —
`#if` above a bare `1` compiles there and takes the branch. The rule is
K816's, and it is what pays for the narrowed condition grammar: K65 is
newline-insensitive, so a condition that may run past its line is a condition
that can swallow the line below. That is exactly what `#if 0` above a
top-level `[ SIDE = 7 ]` did before G5 — it parsed as `0[SIDE = 7]`, an index
into a data section (`postmortem.md` §G.6.4).

Narrowing the grammar removed the *postfix*, which is where the absorption
came from, but not the newline: whitespace is a Tree-sitter `extra` and a
newline is whitespace, so the grammar cannot state the rule and the builder
states it instead. The same gap still reaches `const` and `var`, which is the
`newline-subscript` row.

`K816-E0001` rather than a code of its own: the source really is malformed
K816 rather than legacy syntax being refused, and the message and its two
spans carry the specifics.
