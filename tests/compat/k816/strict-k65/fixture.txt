**The flag itself**, which is the one row in the manifest whose construct is
not in any source: `--strict-k65` is a switch, so its fixture is a build run
with it rather than a program containing it.  `strict yes` in the header is
what turns it on, and it is the only fixture in the corpus that does.

Twelve errors and one warning, in source order.  The twelve have no K65
spelling at all - `segment`, `var dp`, a field list, a field width, an
allocation count, a `$` literal, two width annotations, a call contract, the
`#` of `a + #MASK`, the `,s` of `a = 3,s` and the `&` of `a = &2,x` - and the
warning is the one construct here that compiles in *both* languages:
`* 3` is three `NOP`s in K816 and three **cycles** in the original, which
spends an odd one through `NOP $80` (`instr-nop-wait`, decision 11).

The `#` is last because it is inside the body and the ordering is by span.  It
is worth a line of its own: the original has no `#` anywhere outside its
preprocessor, because `Arg -> Number` *is* the immediate there.  K816 reads
a bare number the same way - `a + 1` adds one in both - so `a + #1` is the
same instruction with a marker K65 does not have, which is what makes the
marker the extension and its absence not one.

The `* 3` opens its body rather than following the `a + #MASK`, and that is
K65's own rule rather than a workaround: the language is newline-insensitive
and `*` is also multiplication, so `a + #MASK` above a `* 3` is the single
immediate `#(MASK * 3)` in both compilers.

Without the flag this source compiles clean, which is the claim the flag rests
on: strict mode reports and changes nothing.  That is checked over the whole
corpus rather than here, by `strict_mode_only_adds_diagnostics` - every fixture
compiled both ways, and the strict run's diagnostics with `K816-E0160` and
`K816-W0161` removed have to equal the ordinary run's.

What the flag reports is not decided in code.  It is the `Strict` column of
`doc/k65-compat-manifest.md`, mirrored by `compat::MANIFEST`, and the two are
compared row for row in both directions - a row marked in the file and missing
from the table is a failure, and so is the reverse.  A second gate closes the
loop from the other end: every row the column marks has to be reported by
something in the corpus, because a row marked in a document and found by
nothing is a promise with no witness.
