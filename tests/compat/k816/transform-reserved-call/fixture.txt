**A refusal inside a transform is a wrong image, not a missing message.**
`evaluator [ rnd() ]` compiled clean before this was found by review after G6,
and emitted `00 01 02` — the bytes the loop generated, with the transform
silently skipped.

The mechanism is worth stating because it will recur.  Analysis reports the
four refused evaluator functions **structurally**, by walking
`reserved_eval_call` nodes, and the interpreter answers one with
`Error::Reported` — "someone else already said this" — so nothing complains at
the point the call runs.  `transform_byte` treats a failed transform as "keep
the byte", which is right for a diagnostic that was reported and wrong when it
was not.  The walk's `Evaluator` arm had been left in the no-op list, under a
comment that said its body must be inspected.  `postmortem.md` §G.8.1.

The fixture is invented rather than measured: `rnd()` is refused by decision
(decision 6), so what the original emits for it is not evidence about anything
K816 decides.  What it pins is that the refusal *arrives*.
