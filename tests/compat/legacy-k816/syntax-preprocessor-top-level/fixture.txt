`#if` / `#else` / `#endif` around top-level elements.

Compiles since G5, and the `#if 1` branch is the one that survives: the
program declares `enabled` and nothing else, and `a=enabled` reads it.  The
`#elif 1` below it is *not* evaluated even though its condition is true,
because the chain has already taken a branch — the rule C has, and the one
place K816 parts company with the original, which evaluates every condition
in a chain whether or not it can decide anything (`preprocessor-dead-region`
measures that, and the `pp-dead-conditions` manifest row records the change).

Four `K816-E0201` stood here before G5, one per directive.  The legacy
compiler never evaluated a directive at all — it warned and skipped the line,
parsing the body either way — so it is not an oracle for anything here; the
original is (`compiler.inc:7-25`, `pp_if` and friends in
`compiler_src.cpp:999`), and G5 implements what it does.
